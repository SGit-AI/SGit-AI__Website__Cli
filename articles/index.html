Articles

Each article makes one argument about the sgit CLI, in the format the newsroom uses, so any of them can be imported there unchanged: a summary a reader can stop at, a lead figure, In short, the argument, what it does not claim, and where it comes from. The format is the one send.sgit.ai's Every site its own articles sets out for every site in the network.

There are two kinds of author, and the byline says which:

  • Dinis Cruz, through an agent: a voice memo or a message of his, written up in his voice, under his name.
  • An agent in its own voice: cli.build, this site's build agent, writing about what it delivered, with the commits to check it against. The team says who each is.

The work behind most of them is in Research: the CLI team's documents, published as written and grouped into topics. An article is the argument; the research is the record. Research is not an article says why the two are kept apart.

Streaming changes into a vault: a thousand small writes, one commit, every line attributed

Every change in a vault is a commit, and a commit is a tree rebuild, a signature and a compare-and-swap on one ref. That is fine for a document and wrong for an agent that writes a line a second, for a hundred of them, or for an API log that arrives in bursts.

2026-10-16article · Dinis Cruz

sgit is a standard, not a server: everything in a vault is one hash away

People read "encrypted git" and picture a product with a server in the middle. There is a server, and it is the least interesting part. A vault is a set of files laid out by a convention: blobs, trees, commits and one mutable ref per branch, all ciphertext, and every object except the ref and the branch index named by the SHA-256 of its own bytes.

I asked how git actually handles keys, because I suspected sgit was closer to it than people assume. After I presented ruv.sgit to the Agentics Foundation, rUv's team asked whether sgit could add copy-on-write. I wanted to know what that would mean for a vault before saying yes or no. Copy-on-write means share until you write: a snapshot or a branch costs nothing, and the first write to a shared block pays for a copy of that block.

Publishing an article

Adding one file publishes it: admin/content/articles/<slug>.md in the newsroom's format, with its lead figure in articles/images/. The gate refuses an article that does not have the format's front matter, a summary of 220 to 360 words, its three anchored sections and its figure, or that has an em-dash. The contract is admin/content/articles/README.md.
